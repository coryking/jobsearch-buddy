# Anduril Industries

Anduril Industries is a private defense technology company founded in 2017, headquartered in Costa Mesa, California. The company builds autonomous systems, AI-powered command and control software, and weapons platforms for the U.S. Department of Defense and allied nations. Palmer Luckey (founder of Oculus VR) serves as co-founder and CTO; Brian Schimpf is CEO and co-founder. Other co-founders include Trae Stephens (formerly of Palantir), Matt Grimm (COO), and Joe Chen. The company has approximately 7,000 employees across offices in Costa Mesa, Seattle, Boston, Washington D.C., Atlanta, London, and Sydney.

Anduril has raised approximately $7 billion in total funding across 14 rounds. Key rounds include a $2.5 billion Series G in June 2025 led by Founders Fund at a $30.5 billion valuation, and a reported $4 billion round in early 2026 led by Andreessen Horowitz and Thrive Capital at a $60 billion valuation. Revenue was approximately $1 billion in 2024 and roughly $2 billion in 2025. Palmer Luckey has stated the company will "definitely" go public but has not committed to a timeline; analyst estimates place a potential IPO in late 2026 or 2027.

## Products and Technology

Anduril's product line spans software, autonomous vehicles, and munitions:

**Lattice** is the company's AI-powered operating system for command and control. It fuses data from thousands of sensors into a real-time 3D operating picture and coordinates autonomous platforms across air, land, sea, and space domains. The $20 billion U.S. Army contract awarded in March 2026 centers on integrating Lattice as a unified procurement platform, consolidating over 120 previously separate procurement actions.

**Autonomous aircraft.** Fury (designated YFQ-44A) is a high-subsonic, jet-class autonomous combat aircraft selected for the U.S. Air Force's Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) program alongside General Atomics, beating out Boeing, Lockheed Martin, and Northrop Grumman. The Air Force plans to acquire at least 1,000 CCA units at approximately $20-30M each. ALTIUS is a family of tube-launched fixed-wing drones acquired through the Area-I acquisition, deployable from C-130s, helicopters, and ground vehicles. Anvil (also called Interceptor) is a counter-drone quadcopter that uses computer vision to ram or detonate against enemy drones; a munitions variant (Anvil-M) was introduced in 2023.

**Munitions.** Barracuda is a family of air-breathing cruise missiles in three variants (100, 250, and 500) with ranges from 160 km to 800 km. Roadrunner is an autonomous interceptor.

**Maritime and undersea.** Ghost Shark is an extra-large autonomous undersea vehicle co-developed with the Australian Defence Force for ISR and strike-support in the Indo-Pacific. Barracuda also has naval launch variants.

**Space.** The March 2026 acquisition of ExoAnalytic Solutions (a firm operating 400+ commercial telescopes for orbital tracking) positions Anduril for the Golden Dome missile defense program and space domain awareness contracts.

## Manufacturing

Arsenal-1 is Anduril's $1 billion, 5-million-square-foot manufacturing campus in Pickaway County, Ohio, roughly 20 miles south of Columbus. As of March 2026, the facility is ahead of schedule and under budget, with Fury production beginning by end of March 2026. Roadrunner interceptors, Barracuda cruise missiles, and hardware for a classified program are expected to follow by year-end. The facility targets 4,000+ jobs at full scale by 2035, starting from approximately 250 in late 2025. Anduril also operates a solid rocket motor factory in Mississippi, targeting annual production of 6,000 tactical motors by end of 2026.

## Business Model and Contracts

Anduril inverts the traditional defense procurement model: rather than waiting for government Requests for Proposals, it self-funds R&D and brings pre-built products to the DoD. This Silicon Valley approach to defense contracting -- build first, sell after -- distinguishes it from traditional primes. Major contracts include:

- **U.S. Army enterprise contract** (March 2026): up to $20 billion over 10 years for Lattice-centered AI battlefield capabilities
- **Air Force CCA program** (2024): one of two vendors selected to develop autonomous combat wingmen
- **Marine Corps counter-drone defense**: $642 million, 10-year contract
- **Golden Dome missile defense**: among early awardees alongside Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, and True Anomaly
- **Rheinmetall partnership**: joint manufacturing of Barracuda, Fury, and solid rocket motors for European defense

## Acquisitions

Anduril has grown through targeted acquisitions: Area-I (tube-launched drones, 2021), Copious Imaging (sensors, 2021), Dive Technologies (undersea vehicles, 2022), and ExoAnalytic Solutions (space surveillance, 2026).

## Competitive Landscape

Anduril occupies a distinct position as a venture-backed defense startup competing against traditional primes (Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, L3Harris) and alongside other defense-tech companies (Palantir, Shield AI). Palantir offers overlapping battlefield data integration capabilities; the two companies participate in Warp Speed, a consortium organized by Palantir that also includes L3Harris and Shield AI. The competitive dynamics are complex -- these firms both compete for contracts and partner on programs.

## Engineering and Technology Stack

The engineering team draws heavily from Silicon Valley backgrounds -- Palantir, Oculus, game development, computer graphics, fintech, and autonomy research rather than the traditional defense-contractor workforce. Primary programming languages are Go (described by employees as "overwhelmingly" the most-used language for non-robotics work), Rust and C++ (robotics and embedded systems), and Haskell. The company uses Nix for reproducible build environments and CI/CD. Anduril maintains 15 public GitHub repositories including SDKs in Python, Go, and JavaScript. The engineering culture leans toward functional programming.

## Organizational Culture and Work Environment

Glassdoor reviews (4.0/5 across 261 reviews, 70% recommend to a friend) and Blind reviews (4.3/5 across 182 reviews) present a company where mission intensity and high expectations are defining characteristics.

**Pace and hours.** Work-life balance is the lowest-rated category on both platforms (3.2/5 on Glassdoor, 3.3/5 on Blind). Reviews consistently describe long hours as the norm. Multiple employees report 55+ hour weeks, being contacted outside work hours, and expectations of constant availability. One reviewer describes it as "high operational tempo; long hours are common and often expected." Another notes being "expected to be available at all times." The company provides a work phone, which employees interpret as both a perk and a signal about availability expectations.

**Mission orientation.** The mission -- national defense and protecting military personnel -- is a strong pull for employees who describe themselves and coworkers as "super mission driven" and "highly motivated." Career growth is the highest-rated category on Blind (4.2/5). Multiple reviews describe smart, humble colleagues who learn quickly regardless of seniority level. One Blind reviewer characterizes the engineering culture as "real and strong" with people who "know what they're doing."

**Management.** Opinions on management are polarized. Some reviews praise autonomy and lack of micromanagement. Others describe management as untrained, with "no clear criteria for placing people in charge of others." Specific criticisms include managers "hiding information from employees" and "openly disparaging people and groups in meetings." Several reviews reference an organizational culture where "arrogance has gone largely unchecked" as the company has grown, with leadership described as crossing professional boundaries. Decision-making is characterized as fast but sometimes at the expense of employee development -- a "quick to fire" pattern appears in multiple reviews.

**Growing pains.** The company is navigating the transition from startup to large organization. Reviews describe a lack of established processes alongside resistance to adopting new ones. Some employees push for structure while others resist bureaucratic policies. Benefits have reportedly been reduced as the company approaches IPO, including elimination of free food and removal of cash bonuses. Glassdoor culture and values rating is 3.7/5, with some employees describing a "cliquey corporate culture" and a disconnect between leadership and rank-and-file employees.

**Compensation.** Blind data shows total compensation ranging from approximately $227K (25th percentile) to $499K (90th percentile) across all roles, with software engineers at $253K to $534K. Multiple reviews note that compensation "papers over the downsides."
