# Walgreens

Walgreens is the largest retail pharmacy chain in the United States, operating approximately 7,960 stores as of early 2026. Founded in 1901 by Charles Walgreen Sr. in Chicago, the company was publicly traded for nearly a century before being taken private in August 2025 when Sycamore Partners completed a $10 billion acquisition ($23.7 billion including debt). Walgreens is headquartered in Deerfield, Illinois, with a technology hub in downtown Chicago that houses an estimated 1,800 employees. The company employs roughly 311,000 people total. It competes primarily with CVS Health (which holds a larger overall market share due to its insurance and PBM businesses), Walmart pharmacy, and Amazon Pharmacy.

## What Walgreens Does

Walgreens fills prescriptions and sells retail health and wellness products. Roughly two-thirds of revenue comes from prescription drug sales, with Walgreens handling about 20% of total U.S. prescription revenue. Annual revenue was $147.7 billion for fiscal year ending August 2024, with trailing twelve-month revenue reaching $154.6 billion by May 2025. Beyond the core pharmacy and retail business, the company invested heavily in primary care through VillageMD (opening 200+ in-store clinics before scaling back after $1.7 billion in operating losses in the healthcare division), and operates Shields Health Solutions (specialty pharmacy) and CareCentrix (post-acute care coordination).

Following the Sycamore acquisition, the former Walgreens Boots Alliance conglomerate was split into five standalone companies: Walgreens (U.S. pharmacy retail), The Boots Group (U.K. pharmacy chain), VillageMD, CareCentrix, and Shields Health Solutions. Mike Motz (formerly CEO of Staples U.S. Retail, a Sycamore portfolio company) was named CEO. John Lederer, a former WBA board director and Sycamore Senior Advisor, became Executive Chairman.

## Technology and Engineering

The Walgreens technology organization supports 8,000+ store locations, processes billions of transactions annually, and manages a loyalty program with 134 million members. The tech stack includes Java, Node/TypeScript, .NET, Python, React on the front end, and a multi-cloud infrastructure spanning Azure (ACS, AKS), Google Cloud, AWS, and IBM. Data infrastructure uses Databricks for lakehouse-style analytics across petabytes of data, with Oracle, MySQL, MongoDB, and Cassandra in the database layer. Mobile is a long-standing focus -- the Walgreens app has been evolving for over a decade with features like refill-by-scan and personalized health triggers.

Tech teams are organized around software engineering, data science, cybersecurity, product management, and program management. The Chicago technology hub is the primary location for engineering roles. A developer portal exists for API partnerships, though there is no visible open-source program or public engineering blog with technical depth.

## Working at Walgreens: What Employees Report

Walgreens has a 3.1/5 overall rating on Glassdoor across 36,836 reviews, with 36% of employees recommending it to a friend and 26% expressing a positive business outlook. On Blind, the rating is also 3.1/5 across 58 reviews. Software engineers rate it slightly higher at 3.4/5 on Glassdoor (44 reviews), with average software engineer compensation reported around $148,000/year.

**Work-life balance** gets mixed signals depending on role type. Blind reviewers rate work-life balance highest at 3.9/5, and multiple reviews describe the environment as "chill" with "nice coworkers." However, Glassdoor reviews from engineering specifically mention a return-to-office mandate that "destroyed work-life balance and caused an exodus of top performers," and inconsistent remote work policies where "some people get to work remotely while others have to come into the office 3 days a week."

**Management and leadership** is the lowest-rated dimension on Blind (2.7/5). Multiple reviews across platforms describe "poor leadership and management," a "blame game culture," and "toxic management" with "no clear vision." The company has undergone substantial leadership churn -- a new CEO (Tim Wentworth) arrived in October 2023, orchestrated the sale to Sycamore, and was replaced by Mike Motz upon the acquisition closing in August 2025. Reviews reference "new unproven leadership at every level" and "a lot of uncertainty going forward."

**Engineering culture** reviews describe Walgreens as "lacking proper engineering culture" with "very inconsistent salary bands" and "few growth opportunities." Multiple reviews cite the company as "slow to adapt to new technology" with "no budget for exciting projects." On the positive side, some describe a "huge learning culture opportunity to learn about claims data, healthcare, and immunization" -- the domain knowledge rather than the engineering practices being the draw.

**Compensation** is rated 2.8/5 company-wide on Glassdoor. Engineering-specific reviews (3.2/5 for comp) indicate somewhat better pay than store-level roles but still describe it as "low pay" relative to tech industry norms, with "recently canceled bonuses" mentioned in 2024 reviews.

## Current State and Trajectory

Walgreens is in active contraction and restructuring. The company announced plans to close 1,200 stores over three years, with 500+ already shuttered by early 2026 and roughly 350 more planned for 2026. Corporate layoffs have been ongoing: 145 employees in January 2024, 646 in March 2024, 256 in December 2024, and 628 more announced in early 2026 (469 in Illinois, 159 in Texas from a distribution center closure). Former CEO Tim Wentworth described the pre-acquisition business model as "non-sustainable" in October 2024.

Under Sycamore Partners' ownership, the restructuring has shifted from what press coverage describes as "gradual right-sizing" to "aggressive surgical removal of unprofitable assets." The company is simultaneously managing a breakup into five standalone entities while closing stores and reducing headcount. Stefano Pessina (former WBA chairman and largest individual shareholder) and his family reinvested 100% of their interests, signaling some continuity of long-term commitment alongside the private equity restructuring.
