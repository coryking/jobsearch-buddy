# Bank of America

Bank of America Corporation (NYSE: BAC) is the second-largest bank in the United States by assets ($3.41 trillion as of 2025) and the second-largest globally by market capitalization, behind JPMorgan Chase. Headquartered in Charlotte, North Carolina, the company employs approximately 213,000 people and serves around 70 million consumer and small business clients plus institutional and corporate clients in over 35 countries. Annual revenue was $191.6 billion in 2025, with net income of $30.5 billion. CEO Brian Moynihan has led the company since 2010.

The modern Bank of America was formed in 1998 when Charlotte-based NationsBank acquired BankAmerica Corporation of San Francisco for $62 billion -- at the time, the largest bank merger in U.S. history. The merged entity took the Bank of America name. The lineage traces further back to the Bank of Italy, founded in San Francisco in 1904 by Amadeo Giannini, and through other predecessors to Massachusetts Bank, chartered in 1784.

## Business Lines and Customers

Bank of America operates through four segments: Consumer Banking (retail banking, consumer lending), Global Wealth and Investment Management (Merrill Lynch Wealth Management for clients with $250K+ in investable assets, Bank of America Private Bank for ultra-high-net-worth clients with $3M+), Global Banking (middle-market commercial banking for companies with $50M-$2B revenue, plus corporate and investment banking for larger firms), and Global Markets (institutional sales and trading across fixed income, currencies, commodities, and equities). Its primary competitors are JPMorgan Chase, Wells Fargo, and Citigroup.

## Technology and AI Investment

Bank of America spends $13 billion annually on technology overall, with approximately $4 billion directed toward new strategic technology initiatives in 2025 -- a 44% increase over the past decade. The bank holds nearly 7,000 granted patents and pending patent applications, the most of any financial services company, with nearly 1,100 of those in AI and machine learning (a 94% increase since 2022).

The bank's flagship consumer AI product is Erica, a virtual financial assistant launched in 2018 that has surpassed 3 billion client interactions from nearly 50 million users, averaging 58 million interactions per month. The underlying natural language processing engine follows an "invest once, reuse everywhere" approach and now powers AI agents across wealth management (Merrill), corporate banking (CashPro), and benefits administration. As of 2025, the bank reports 90% of its workforce actively uses AI tools, and it won Forrester's 2025 Data and AI Impact Award.

The technology stack includes significant legacy in COBOL and .NET alongside Java, with some Python adoption. The bank has used OpenStack for private cloud infrastructure. It is a member of FINOS (Fintech Open Source Foundation) through its secur ities division. There is no public engineering blog; technology communication happens primarily through press releases, conference appearances, and the bank's annual Global Technology Conference.

## Technology Hub Locations

The global technology and operations division, employing over 20,000 people, is based at Charlotte headquarters. The bank maintains five central hubs: Charlotte, New York City, London, Hong Kong, and Toronto, with additional presence in Dublin (European headquarters for corporate and investment banking), Singapore, and Minneapolis. Investment banking and trading operations concentrate in New York City's Bank of America Tower.

## Organizational Culture and Work Environment

Glassdoor reviews (3.9/5 across 42,679 reviews, with 75% recommending to a friend) and Blind posts paint a consistent picture of a large, stable institution with strong benefits but significant bureaucratic overhead.

**Pace and process.** Multiple employee reviews describe Bank of America as "slow-moving" with "slow promotions." Decision-making involves multiple management layers and approval processes. Reviews on Glassdoor specifically reference "puzzling bureaucracy" and "bloated bureaucracy." Software engineers on Glassdoor (72% recommend, down 7% over 12 months) describe technology as "5 years behind" in some areas, though others note a "progressive engineering culture with emphasis on up-skilling and using new technologies." The disconnect suggests the experience varies substantially by team and business line.

**Stability and benefits.** The recurring positives across review platforms are benefits (401k match up to 5%, RSUs, health coverage), job security, vacation time, and work-life balance (rated 3.8/5 on Glassdoor). Multiple reviews describe it as "a good place to stay comfortable, not to stay ambitious." The company is not in hypergrowth -- headcount has held steady at approximately 213,000 since 2023, down from 218,000 through attrition rather than mass layoffs. CEO Moynihan indicated headcount is expected to decline modestly in 2026.

**Career growth.** Career opportunities are rated 3.7/5 on Glassdoor, the lowest of their subcategories. Reviews consistently note limited growth opportunities within a given team, with advancement often requiring a team or division change. Some reviews on Blind describe "below-market compensation" relative to other Wall Street firms and tech companies, with favoritism influencing promotion decisions.

**Remote work.** Bank of America has moved firmly toward in-office work. The bank issued "letters of education" to employees not meeting in-office attendance requirements in early 2024, and has continued tightening return-to-office expectations through 2025, with five days per week reported as the expectation for many roles. Internal reports from late 2024 suggested that resistance to return-to-office policies was a factor in workforce reduction targeting.

**Compensation for technology roles.** According to Levels.fyi, software engineer total compensation ranges from roughly $112K (Software Engineer 1) to $159K (Software Engineer 3), with Glassdoor reporting a broader range up to approximately $232K at the 90th percentile. This positions BofA below top-tier tech companies and some competing banks for technology talent, though the benefits package partially offsets the gap.

**Communication and management.** Reviews mention lack of communication between upper management and engineering teams (SCRUM teams), with project cancellations sometimes coming without warning. Some reviews note disparities in treatment and expectations between U.S. and India-based employees.

## Leadership and Strategy

Moynihan's stated strategy centers on "Responsible Growth" -- growing revenue within a risk framework in a sustainable manner. In September 2025, he named Dean Athanasia and Jim DeMare as Co-Presidents to drive company-wide initiatives on cost management, market share growth, and AI development. CTIO Hari Gopalkrishnan, a 14-year company veteran promoted in mid-2025, steers technology investment. Moynihan has emphasized bringing in external perspectives on AI, consulting professors, practitioners, and vendors alongside internal capabilities.

## Workforce Dynamics

The bank has been managing headcount downward through attrition since 2023, with targeted cuts in specific areas -- 150 junior investment banking positions in early 2025, 20 investment bankers in Asia in 2024, and selective reductions focused on what the bank described as high-paid, low-performing employees and automation-vulnerable roles. No mass layoff events have been announced, but the trajectory is a gradual contraction as automation and AI absorb certain functions.
